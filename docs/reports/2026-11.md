## Executive summary

Five previously untracked, source-supported developments were added to the cumulative monitoring database, bringing the total from **28 to 33 events**. The new entries cover (1) a proposed commercial drone-delivery ordinance withdrawn before adoption in Belvedere, California; (2) a Wing/Walmart special-use permit that did not advance in Kirkwood, Missouri; (3) Wentzville, Missouri's first reading of conditional-use rules; (4) Tracy, California's announced public town hall for Amazon Prime Air; and (5) the pending federal Part 108/BVLOS rulemaking. The existing Zipline Texas Draft EA entry was also corrected to show that the October 11 comment period **closed**, rather than remaining open.

These records reinforce a distinction central to drone-delivery land-use planning: **ground infrastructure may require municipal permits or land-use decisions even when FAA operational authorization exists**. Outcomes vary. Kirkwood's proposed site did not advance, Wentzville was still developing its code, and Belvedere withdrew a proposed ordinance before the final vote. None of these should be recorded as a blanket prohibition on drone overflight.

## New verified developments

### 1. Belvedere, California: drone-delivery ordinance withdrawn (October 12)

Belvedere's mayor withdrew the proposed commercial drone-delivery ordinance from second reading at the October 12 City Council meeting. The council received public comment but did not discuss or vote on final adoption. The ordinance had received a 3–2 first-reading vote on September 14. Local reporting on October 20 described resident opposition, including calls for a ban, and the city's intention to explore a coordinated approach with other Marin County communities. **The ordinance was not adopted.** This corrects contrary secondary reports that had described the October 12 meeting as final passage.

Source: [The Ark, October 20](https://www.thearknewspaper.com/live/belvedere-shelves-rules-for-drone-deliveries-after-resident-backlash).

### 2. Kirkwood, Missouri: proposed Wing/Walmart site fails to advance (October 15)

The Kirkwood City Council considered a special-use permit for a Wing/Walmart drone-delivery facility at 1202 South Kirkwood Road. At the October 15 meeting, no member made a motion to advance the bill, and the proposal did not proceed. Residents raised noise, privacy and wildlife concerns. **This is coded as non-advancement without a motion, not a recorded unanimous denial**: some accounts described a vote, while contemporaneous KMOX reporting and a separate detailed account describe the absence of a motion. The City of Kirkwood's meeting calendar independently documents the original SUP-08-26 application.

Sources: [KMOX, October 16](https://www.audacy.com/kmox/news/local/walmart-drone-delivery-dead-in-kirkwood); [City of Kirkwood permit subcommittee notice](https://www.kirkwoodmo.org/Home/Components/Calendar/Event/15930/); [First Alert 4, October 1 hearing](https://www.firstalert4.com/2026/10/02/kirkwood-hosts-public-hearing-walmart-drone-delivery-permit-request/).

### 3. Wentzville, Missouri: conditional-use regulation receives first reading (October 21)

Wentzville's Board of Aldermen gave first-reading approval to a proposal requiring drone-delivery facilities to obtain conditional-use permits, show FAA authorization, carry insurance and perform noise testing at launch sites and nearby homes. The first reading was confirmed to local media by the mayor. The measure is **not yet an adopted ordinance**, and the action did not authorize a particular Wing/Walmart nest. Further consideration was expected in early November.

Sources: [First Alert 4, October 22](https://www.firstalert4.com/2026/10/22/wentzville-considers-new-rules-drone-delivery-sites/); [DroneXL review of municipal agenda materials, October 22](https://dronexl.co/2026/10/22/wentzville-wing-walmart-drone-delivery-bill-4914/).

### 4. Tracy, California: proposed Prime Air service enters public engagement (October 22)

The City of Tracy posted an official notice for an October 22 public town hall featuring Amazon Prime Air representatives to discuss proposed delivery service and receive questions and feedback. The notice establishes a scheduled municipal engagement opportunity, **not** that a service launch occurred or that any facility permit was granted. A post-meeting municipal outcome was not verified in this sweep.

Source: [City of Tracy event notice](https://www.cityoftracy.org/I-Want-To/View/City-Calendar/2026/Events-Folder/Tracy-Community-Town-Hall-Amazon-Drone-Delivery).

### 5. Federal BVLOS/Part 108 rule: still pending (status checked October 29)

The federal Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs continued to list RIN 2120-AL82, *Normalizing Unmanned Aircraft Systems Beyond Visual Line of Sight Operations*, as **Pending Review** in its late-October public register. OIRA received the final-rule-stage action July 10, 2026. A final rule had not been verified as published by the October 29 check. This matters for scaling delivery networks, but a pending regulatory review does not itself authorize new BVLOS operations.

Sources: [OIRA regulatory review dashboard](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/jsp/EO/eoListingChart.jsp); [OIRA October 29 meeting record](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eom12866SearchResults?pubId=202304&rin=2120-AL82&viewRule=true).

## Interpretation for aviation and land-use planning

1. **Procedural outcomes must be coded precisely.** A first reading is not adoption, a withdrawn ordinance is not a regulation, and a proposal that receives no motion is not necessarily a recorded permit denial.
2. **Community concerns extend beyond acoustics.** Kirkwood's testimony and Belvedere's debate involve privacy, wildlife, hours of operation and confidence in local authority, alongside noise.
3. **Ground-side controls are increasingly the operational bottleneck.** Wentzville's proposed conditional-use review and Kirkwood's stalled site illustrate the importance of siting and local entitlement even when operators already possess FAA certificates.
4. **A live dashboard is not a census.** These 33 entries represent source-supported developments, not all proposed hubs, all affected cities, or a representative count of complaints. Event counts should not be interpreted as incidence rates.

## Verification and reproducibility notes

Sources were selected using targeted October 2026 searches of FAA and federal regulatory registers, municipal pages, local reporting, legal case coverage and operator developments, then cross-checked against existing `docs/data/events.json` identifiers to avoid duplicates. The October 27 repository snapshot contained 28 events; five unique events were added and one prior status was corrected. A primary-source preference was applied, with direct local reporting used when the municipal decision record could not be reliably retrieved. Conflicting descriptions of Kirkwood's procedure were resolved conservatively in favor of the directly reported absence of a motion.

**Limitations:** Municipal archives vary in availability; not every council agenda or court docket is indexed. Public complaints are self-selected and cannot establish population-level acceptance. Operators' proposed maximum flight volumes should not be mistaken for measured flight counts. Pending administrative reviews and lawsuits must not be reported as final outcomes.
